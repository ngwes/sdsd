# SDSD — Stupid-Driven Software Development

> *"The best code is the one that survives contact with stakeholders."*

## What is SDSD?

**Stupid-Driven Software Development (SDSD)** is a pragmatic framework of practices, patterns, and methodologies designed for software developers operating in high-entropy contexts: environments where stakeholders, domain experts, analysts, and the business are unable to define clear requirements, don't understand the product they are requesting, change their minds frequently, and — in the worst case — attribute the responsibility for their own failures to the development team.

SDSD is not cynicism. It is **defensive engineering applied to the human dimension of software**. It is the set of tools a mature professional uses to protect:

- the **quality of the product** from poorly managed external interference;
- the **project** from scope creep, volatile requirements, and structural ambiguity;
- the **development team** from unfounded criticism, retaliation, and arbitrary blame attribution;
- the **economic value** generated by software from irrational decisions.

## Quick Start: The 10 Golden Rules of SDSD

For those in a hurry, these are the highest-impact practices:

1. **Write everything down.** Any verbal decision is as if it never existed.
2. **Get requirements signed off.** An unapproved requirement is not a requirement.
3. **Use an ADR for every relevant architectural decision.**
4. **Define DoD and DoR before starting every sprint.**
5. **No change without a formal Change Request.**
6. **Frequent demos.** Those who don't see, invent.
7. **Create a shared vocabulary (Ubiquitous Language) with the business.**
8. **Trace requirements end-to-end** (RTM: Requirements Traceability Matrix).
9. **Automate verification** — tests are legal proof of conformance.
10. **Document your assumptions** — making them explicit is more powerful than hiding them.

## Philosophical Note

SDSD does not assume bad faith from stakeholders. It assumes that **incompetence, ambiguity, and the inability to articulate complex needs are normal conditions**, not exceptions. A doctor does not complain about patients who cannot make their own diagnosis. A lawyer does not complain about clients who don't know the law. A professional software developer does not complain about stakeholders who don't understand software: **they equip themselves.**
